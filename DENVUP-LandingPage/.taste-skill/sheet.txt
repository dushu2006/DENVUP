This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The name is the headline. Set it so large that it becomes architecture: as wide as the viewport, cropped by an edge, or stacked to fill the screen. Nothing else on the first screen competes; the description of what this is drops to a single small sentence. The idea comes from treating the name's letters as material. Ask what the subject can do to its own name: a photograph of the work sits inside a counter or replaces a letter, the name repeats down the page like a reel, the letters are split to opposite corners and the page happens between them. In the study this was the single most common device among sites scoring 7.5 or more.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the wordmark spans 88–100% of the viewport width (font-size 16–24vw, set with a clamp so it never wraps), anchored either to the bottom edge with its descenders nearly touching it, or dead centre with everything else pushed to the corners. One line of supporting text at 16–20px sits in a corner on a shared margin; navigation is three or four words in the opposite corner. After the hero the page is deliberately small and calm: a narrow text column or a simple two-column grid, so the contrast with the monument holds. The name returns once more at full width as the footer, in the inverse colours. Margins are tight (20–32px) so the type reaches the edges. Two type sizes do nearly all the work: the monument and a small text size, with one mid size for section headings.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. One family. The display cut must survive enormous sizes: tight tracking (-0.04 to -0.07em), leading 0.8–0.9, optical kerning on. Section headings are modest (28–40px) on purpose.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Two values carry the page (ground and ink). The accent, if used at all, appears on one thing only: the single action or the inset image frame. The footer inverts ground and ink.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node /home/user/DENVUP/AI_Skills_Pack/skills/ui-ux-design/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "studio portfolio"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Few images, used as inserts in the typography rather than as illustrations beside it: one photograph inside or behind the name (a 4:5 or 1:1 frame replacing a letter, or tucked between two words), then a short run of large full-width or half-width photographs further down. Square corners. No collage of small thumbnails.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Film title, bottom edge
One photograph fills the viewport at 100vw by 100svh, `object-fit: cover`, with its subject in the upper two-thirds and a calmer, darker zone along the bottom. The name is one condensed uppercase word, or a short name in a high-contrast serif, set at clamp(150px, 17.5vw, 290px) and sized to span the full width between 32px margins. It is centred and anchored so its baseline sits 34px above the bottom edge, covering roughly the lowest quarter of the frame. A soft neutral dark scrim lies under that bottom band only, about 40% of the height; the rest of the image is untouched. Directly above the name's left end, on the same margin, sits the supporting sentence at 16px on two short lines. Directly above its right end, right-aligned, sits the single action as a small paper-coloured rectangular button. The top edge carries a small emblem or wordmark at the left margin and up to five text links at 15px grouped at the right, all in white. The central half of the screen belongs to the view.

On a phone: The photograph is re-cropped to portrait with `object-position` on its subject and fills 100svh. The name stays on the bottom edge at 22vw on one line, or two lines at 20vw beyond eight characters, 20px above the edge. The supporting sentence sits above the name's left end at 15px. The action button moves to the top-right corner and the links collapse to a menu word beside the emblem.
Headline: The name of the place: one word of up to about 12 characters at 17.5vw, or two words stacked on a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Title, note, facts row (split)
First row: a heading of two to four words in a light weight at clamp(44px, 5vw, 76px) sits on the left margin in columns 1–6; a three-line paragraph at 16px, 260px wide, sits in columns 10–12 with its last line on the heading's baseline. Columns 7–9 stay empty. 120px lower, a second row sets three or four facts (a date, a time, a place, a price) as large light text at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px), spread across the 12 columns with `justify-content: space-between`. Each fact has a one-line explanation at 14px directly under it in the soft ink, left-aligned to the fact. Facts are separated only by the space between them, so the row reads like the line of times on a printed programme. A photograph may start under the right half of the facts row, 5 columns wide at 4:3. Padding 140px top, 120px bottom.
On a phone: Heading at 11vw on the left margin; the note follows directly under it at 16px, full width. The facts become a two-column grid with a 32px row gap, each fact at 26px with its explanation beneath. Any photograph goes last, full width.

### 2. about — Side facts, wide paragraph (table)
Two rows share one vertical line at 34% of the viewport width. Row one: in a 160px column at the left margin, three label and value pairs stack with 20px between them, each a label in the soft ink with its value beneath in full ink, all at 13px (founded, based in, team size). From the 34% line a five-line paragraph at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), leading 1.35, runs to 90% of the width. Row two starts 120px lower and repeats the logic for the record of the practice: one plain word at 13px at the far left (awards, press, clients), then from the 34% line two list columns, each 260px wide with a 64px gutter, at 13px with 10px between lines: name at the left, year at the right in the soft ink. No heading; the paragraph opens the section. Each list comfortably holds 6–12 lines. No photographs are needed.
On a phone: Everything aligns to the left margin. The paragraph comes first at 19px. The three label and value pairs follow as a two-column grid at 14px. The two record lists stack with 40px between them, each opened by its plain word in full ink.

### 3. work — Big names, fixed preview (index)
Project names are the index. In columns 1–8, five to nine names stack one per line at clamp(44px, 5.4vw, 80px), leading 1.0, each row a link with 10px of vertical padding. All names rest in the ink at 35% opacity except the active one, which is full ink; the first is active by default, and hover or keyboard focus moves the state. To the right of each name, on its baseline at 14px: the discipline and the year. In columns 9–12 a `position: sticky; top: 15vh` frame, 4:5 and 26vw wide with square corners, shows the active project's photograph; all images are stacked in the frame and swapped by opacity over 200ms. A two-word heading at 28px sits top-left, 48px above the first name. Nothing is drawn between rows: the size of the type and the dimming do the separating.
On a phone: Names at 9.5vw, all in full ink, one per line; discipline and year sit beneath each name at 13px. There is no sticky frame: each name is preceded by its own photograph at 16:10, full width, so the list becomes image then name, repeated, with 40px between projects.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.

### 4. how-it-works — Lines that step across (statement)
The sequence is written, not counted. Four lines of display type, one stage per line, each a complete short clause that begins with its order word: 'First you send it.' 'Then we take it apart.' 'You choose one.' 'It ships on Friday.' Set at clamp(36px, 4.6vw, 76px), leading 1.0, one line each. The first line starts on column 1 and each following line starts two columns further right (grid-column-start 1, 3, 5, 7), so the block descends as a staircase and the eye follows the diagonal. Under each line, on that line's own left edge and 12px below, a sentence of up to 34ch at 17px in the soft ink. 56px between stages. The last line may take the accent colour as the single emphasis. The first line serves as the heading; there is no title above. Leave the columns to the left of the final stage empty.
On a phone: Lines drop to 8.4vw and may wrap onto a second line. The staircase becomes a smaller indent of 6vw per stage, so the fourth line starts 18vw in. Each sentence sits under its line at 16px on the same indent. 40px between stages.

### 5. features — One-size name columns (index)
Pure text at one size. Three columns start on columns 1, 4 and 7 of the 12-column grid, leaving columns 10–12 empty on purpose. Each column opens with a group name in full ink and continues with a tight stack of six to ten capability names in the soft ink, all at 16–17px, regular weight, line-height 1.25, no bullets and no symbols; one blank line separates group name from stack. Hovering a name brings it to full ink and shows a one-line description in a fixed spot: the top of the empty columns 10–12, at the same size. The heading is no larger than 28px; two or three words, alone on column 1, 96px above the columns. Give the section 20vh of empty ground above and below, so the smallness reads as decided. Works on light or deep grounds.
On a phone: Columns stack with 40px between groups; text rises to 18px with line-height 1.4 for touch. Group names stay in full ink. Hover descriptions are dropped; each group ends instead with one sentence in the soft ink that sums it up. The heading is 24px, 48px above.

### 6. gallery — Small frames, wide space (strip)
A flat, quiet viewport 100svh tall in which about 70% of the screen is empty. At mid-height a single row of small portrait frames runs across and off both sides: each frame 11vw wide and 24svh tall (roughly 2:3), spaced 5vw apart, vertically centred, six or seven visible at 1440. Frames have square corners and sit directly on the ground with no border. The row scrolls sideways by drag or trackpad (`overflow-x: auto` with the scrollbar hidden) and may hold 12–24 frames. One line of text overlaps the top-left of the first frame by 12px: a title of two or three words at 20–24px followed by the count, placed above the frame in the stacking order. Hovering a frame scales it to 1.1 from its centre over 250ms; nothing else moves. No captions under frames, no arrows, and no other text in the section.
On a phone: The row sits at 45% of an 80svh section. Frames are 34vw wide at 2:3 and 5vw apart, so two and a half are visible and the row scrolls by touch with snapping. The title line sits 20px above the row at the left margin.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 7. pricing — One plan as a sentence (statement)
The whole offer is one sentence of five to seven short words that contains the price, set at 8–9vw across the full width on two lines, leading 0.95, flush left; it replaces any heading. The price is not styled as a different word. Instead it sits on a filled block of the accent colour exactly one line-height tall with 0.15em of padding at each side, so the figure reads as an object in the line. Below, on one shared baseline 64px under the sentence: a supporting sentence at 20px in columns 1–6 saying what the price includes, and one rectangular button right-aligned in columns 10–12. If a second option exists (annual, team), it is a plain text link under the button, not a second panel. The section is one flat colour field at least 80svh tall.
On a phone: The sentence is set at 11vw on two lines, with the price block on a line of its own between them, as wide as its figure. The supporting sentence follows at 17px, then a full-width button, then the text link for the second option.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 8. faq — Wide heading, indented questions (list)
A heading of three to five words at about 5.5vw runs from the left margin on one or two lines and is the only large thing in the section. 80px beneath it the questions begin, indented to start at column 5 and running to column 12, so the left third under the heading stays empty. Each question is a row at 22–24px in the regular weight with a chevron of two 2px strokes at the far right; rows are separated by 36px of space and no rules. Opening a row lays a tile of the one-step tone behind that row and its answer (24px padding, extending 24px beyond the text on both sides) and shows the answer at 17px with a 48ch measure. Six questions. Nothing else shares the screen; the indent against the wide heading is the composition. Build with `<details>`.
On a phone: The heading sits at 10vw on two lines. Questions lose the indent and run full width at 19px with 28px between rows; the open row keeps its tone tile, now bleeding to both screen edges, with the answer at 16px inside it.

### 9. contact — The email as display type (statement)
The section is 100svh in the inverse of the page's colours. A single email address is set as one line of display type spanning the content width: the font-size is fitted so the address runs from margin to margin (about 7–9vw for 20 characters; break after the @ onto two lines if it is longer than 26), light or regular weight, tight tracking, sitting on a baseline 12vh above the bottom edge. It is a `mailto:` link; on hover the whole line takes the accent ink. In the top-left corner, a sentence of up to 12 words at 20–22px says what to write about. In the two bottom corners at 14px: phone and city at the left, two or three social links at the right. No heading and no form. For a pinned ending, make the section `position: sticky; bottom: 0` beneath the page so the previous section lifts off it.
On a phone: The address breaks after the @ onto two lines at 11.5vw, left-aligned, sitting at 55% of an 86svh screen. The sentence sits at the top-left at 18px. Phone, city and social links stack at the bottom-left at 15px with 12px between them.

### 10. footer — Name first, places under it (list)
The wordmark opens the footer instead of closing it. On a full-bleed field in the page's inverse or final chapter colour, the name spans the full content width as the first thing, its cap line pushed 8% above the footer's top edge so the tops of the letters are sliced off by the section (`overflow: hidden` and a negative top margin in `em`). 72px beneath, a two-part row: columns 1–5 list the places or ways to reach the studio (cities, or email and telephone), one per line at 32–36px in the accent ink, each a link; columns 7–12 hold three columns of small links at 15px, top-aligned with the first place. A final row 96px lower carries the legal line at the left and a back-to-top text link at the right, both 13px. The field is about 90svh tall, and nothing sits between the wordmark and the row but space.
On a phone: The sliced wordmark stays at the top, fitted to the width. Places follow as a list at 26px, then the link columns two across at 15px. Legal line and back-to-top link share the last row at 12px, at opposite margins.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.
- It crops letters along their top: the inspector fails that (jurors read it as broken). Crop at the bottom edge or a side instead.

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 9. Where the richness comes from
Scale. The size of the name against the smallness of everything else is the entire visual event, so protect both ends: do not shrink the name to make room, and do not let body text creep above 20px.

## 10. How this direction goes wrong
A long name set small is just a logo. If the name has more than about 12 characters, stack it on two lines or crop it with the viewport edge; never drop below 12vw. Do not add a second big headline under the name. Do not decorate the letters.

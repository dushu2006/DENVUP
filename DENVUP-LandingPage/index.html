The web has enough average.

We are a small studio with an unfashionable belief: a website should be made deliberately, for one business, by the people who will answer the phone when it breaks.

  1. A website is a place.

    People arrive, look around and decide how they feel before they read a word. We design that arrival — the light, the pace, the first thing that moves.

  2. Motion is a sentence.

    It can explain a change, carry a hierarchy or set a tempo. If it does none of those, it is noise with a performance cost. We cut it.

  3. Speed is a design decision.

    Weight is chosen at the beginning: fewer typefaces, honest images, effects that earn their kilobytes. A slow site is not a hosting problem; it is a series of design decisions.

  4. Access is the floor.

    Contrast, keyboard paths, reduced motion and honest form states are how the thing works, not a compliance layer added at the end. If a technique cannot be used by everyone, it gets a fallback or it gets cut.

  5. Specific beats impressive.

    We would rather build something that fits you exactly than something that could belong to anyone. That usually means fewer sections and sharper ones.

That is the whole studio. Four people, one standard.

Six stages, no theatre.

A project moves through the same six stages. You always know which one we are in, what is being decided, and what you will hold at the end of it.

  1. A conversation, then a written brief: goals, audience, content, constraints and what success looks like. We ask the uncomfortable questions while they are still cheap to answer.

    You end this stage with Brief, success measures, honest fit

    Usually one call and one round of written notes. If we are not the right studio, we say so here.

  2. Sitemap, page roles, content plan, technical approach and a realistic sequence. You see the shape of the site before anyone designs a pixel.

    You end this stage with Sitemap, scope, sequence, unknowns

    We separate what is known from what is still a guess, and put the guesses where they can be tested.

  3. Art direction first, then page designs and motion tests. Risky moments are prototyped rather than described in a slide.

    You end this stage with Direction, page designs, a working prototype

    You review at real widths, on your own phone, with real content wherever it exists.

  4. Built in the open: semantic HTML, design tokens, component styles and motion with reduced-motion paths. Reviews happen on real devices, not only in a design file.

    You end this stage with Working build and a staging link

    Progress is visible. Nothing is revealed for the first time on launch day.

  5. Keyboard paths, labels, contrast, breakpoints, slow connections, WebGL fallbacks and every form state. We fix what we find before you do.

    You end this stage with Test notes, fixes, known limitations

    The testing report for this site is published, including the things that failed and why.

  6. Domains, hosting, metadata, redirects and analytics. Then a walkthrough, written documentation and clean handover of every account and file.

    You end this stage with Live site, documentation, accounts

    You leave owning the site and the path to change it — not dependent on us to edit a sentence.

The things worth asking first.

Pricing, process, ownership and what we will not pretend to know. Search the list or open a question.

What does a project cost?

Landing pages start at ₹15,000, business websites at ₹20,000 and advanced custom work at ₹50,000. The final price depends on scope, complexity, functionality and content. We give a written estimate after the first conversation.

How long does a project take?

A landing page is typically one to two weeks, a business site two to four, and custom work four to eight or more. Content readiness and feedback speed move that number more than anything else. Any timeline we give is a planning estimate until scope is signed off.

Do you work with businesses outside India?

Yes. We work remotely and schedule calls across time zones. Everything is documented in writing so the project does not depend on overlapping office hours.

Who writes the content?

You can, and it usually produces the most honest site. We can also structure, edit or write the copy as part of the scope. Content is collected in a single document before design is locked.

Can you work with our existing brand?

Yes. We work inside existing guidelines, or extend them where the web needs rules the brand book never covered: motion, focus states, responsive type. If there is no system yet, we can build one.

What do you need from us to start?

One decision-maker, your content or access to it, and any brand assets. Then honesty about budget and timing. We will bring the questions, the plan and the first prototype.

Do we own the website when it is finished?

Ownership and licensing are set out in the proposal before work begins. Wherever possible the site is built on your accounts — your domain, your hosting, your repository — so access does not depend on us.

What about domains, hosting and third-party costs?

Domains, hosting, API usage, telephony and other third-party services may be billed separately, usually directly to you and in your name. We help set them up and document what each one costs.

Will the site work on phones?

It is designed on a phone first and checked at 360, 768, 1280 and 1600 pixels. Hover interactions get touch equivalents, horizontal sections become usable vertical ones, and nothing essential hides behind a pointer.

Is the site accessible?

We work to WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline: contrast, keyboard paths, visible focus, labelled controls, reduced motion and real error messages. We document what was tested. A formal third-party audit can be arranged and quoted separately.

Can you maintain the site after launch?

Yes, either as a monthly care arrangement or as occasional fixed-scope work. Handover always includes documentation so you are not dependent on us for basic edits.

Do you use templates or page builders?

No page builders. We build with HTML, CSS, TypeScript and specific libraries where they earn their weight. That is why the effects on this site can be tuned rather than switched on.

What happens on the first call?

Thirty minutes: what you are making, what it needs to do, what exists already and what a realistic budget is. You will leave with a straight answer about fit, even if the answer is no.

What we check before calling it done.

This page makes claims about accessibility and speed. Here is the list we hold ourselves to, and the report we publish when something fails.

  • Keyboard, end to end

    Every control reachable, focus visible, dialogs trap and restore focus, no keyboard traps.

  • Reduced motion

    A full content path with animation off; parallax, pinning and cursor effects disabled, not just shortened.

  • Contrast and colour

    Text and control contrast checked against WCAG 2.2 AA; state never communicated by colour alone.

  • Real widths

    360, 768, 1280 and 1600 pixels with no horizontal overflow at any of them.

  • Touch equivalents

    Hover-only information gets a tap path; horizontal rails scroll with a finger or offer arrows.

  • Slow and offline

    Tested against a slow connection and with WebGL disabled; content remains usable if a library fails.

  • Honest states

    Loading, empty, error and success states correspond to something that actually happened.

  • Published results

    TESTING_REPORT.md lists the checks, the failures and what remains unverified in this environment.

The full report ships next to the code in this repository. Report a problem.

Accessibility

Accessibility statement

This site is built to WCAG 2.2 AA as its baseline: semantic landmarks, one main heading per section, visible focus, keyboard-operable controls, labelled fields, reduced-motion layouts and text contrast checked by hand.

Motion is optional. A full reduced-motion path keeps every section and all content, removes pinning, parallax, cursor effects and auto-playing movement, and stops decorative canvases.

The contact form in this build has no server endpoint, so it cannot store or transmit your message. It says so, and gives you a prefilled email and WhatsApp instead. No cookies are set and no third-party analytics or trackers load.

If something does not work for you, email denvupforyou@gmail.com with the page and what happened. That is the fastest route to a fix.

Tested items and known limitations are listed in TESTING_REPORT.md in the repository.

Privacy

Privacy, in plain language

This website sets no cookies and loads no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking scripts. Fonts and assets are served from this site, not a font CDN.

If you use the contact form in this build, nothing is transmitted: there is no endpoint. The email and WhatsApp links open your own mail or messaging application, and the message is sent by you, through that provider, under their terms.

If you email or message DENVUP, your details are used to reply to that enquiry and for nothing else. Ask us to delete the conversation and we will.

This page is a statement of how this build behaves, not a legal contract.

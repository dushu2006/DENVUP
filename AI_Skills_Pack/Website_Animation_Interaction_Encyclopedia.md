# Website Animation & Interaction Encyclopedia
## A practical reference for modern UI/UX, motion design, and creative frontend development

**Purpose:** A broad menu of techniques for designers and coding agents. These are options, not a checklist. Choose techniques that support the content, brand, usability, accessibility, and performance.

**Legend**
- **CSS**: usually possible with CSS transitions/keyframes.
- **JS**: needs JavaScript or an interaction/animation library.
- **SVG**: vector paths and shapes.
- **Canvas**: pixel/particle drawing.
- **3D/WebGL**: GPU-powered 3D or shader experiences.
- **Media**: image, video, or audio assets may be needed.

## 22. How to choose the right effects

For each section, decide:
1. What information must the visitor understand?
2. What interaction genuinely helps communicate it?
3. What is the section's signature effect?
4. How does it transition into the next section?
5. Does it work with mouse, touch, keyboard, and reduced motion?
6. Does it load quickly and perform well on mobile?
7. Is the effect original and intentional, or just decoration?
8. Is the underlying content still clear if the effect fails?

### Suggested DENVUP mapping
- **Navigation:** active-section indicator + carefully animated mobile menu.
- **Hero:** short brand reveal + one signature visual (CSS/SVG or optimized 3D).
- **Capabilities:** interactive concept gallery with distinct visual experiments.
- **Services:** service selector that changes its diagram or artwork.
- **About:** kinetic editorial typography and a transforming brand graphic.
- **Process:** scroll-aware connected timeline.
- **Technologies:** interactive technical atlas, listing only tools the team actually uses.
- **Team:** real portraits when available, with image masks and restrained hover motion.
- **Pricing:** clear comparison and optional scope estimator.
- **FAQ:** accessible accordion.
- **Contact:** validated form, honest loading/success/error states, working email/WhatsApp alternatives.
- **Footer:** animated brand signature and clear navigation.

## 23. Non-negotiable rules
- Do not use every effect just because it appears in this list.
- Give every major section a distinct signature, but keep one coherent design system.
- Do not fabricate client work, testimonials, achievements, statistics, team portraits, or project outcomes.
- Never show “success” unless an action actually succeeded.
- Avoid long forced intros, autoplay sound, scroll-jacking, flashing effects, and cursor tricks that obstruct controls.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and provide keyboard/touch alternatives.
- Test desktop, mobile, performance, accessibility, and all interactive states.
- Keep content usable without animation.
- Treat this encyclopedia as a menu of possibilities, not a mandatory checklist.

**Core instruction for a coding agent:** Audit the existing website, choose the most appropriate techniques from this library, invent better ideas where useful, implement them directly, test them, and remove effects that feel repetitive, inaccessible, slow, or unrelated to the content.
